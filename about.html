The Story

About This Project

A closer look at why the Palm Treo 650 still matters, and the people who built this tribute site.

This site exists to document one of the devices that quietly set the template for the modern smartphone. Long before the term was common, the Treo 650 was already merging a phone, an organizer, a browser and a keyboard into a single pocket-sized handset — the exact combination that later devices refined and popularized.

Palm's Treo line began with the Treo 600 in 2003, folding the company's earlier PDA software into a phone for the first time. The Treo 650 arrived a year later with a sharper screen, a faster processor, a removable battery, and an expansion slot — fixing most of the 600's rough edges while keeping the keyboard-first design people liked.

We built this site as a student project to practice real front-end work — layout, typography, motion and interaction — while researching and presenting a device that deserves more attention than it usually gets in smartphone history.

Palm's Road to the Treo 650

A short timeline of how the device came to be.

1996

The Original Palm Pilot

Palm launches the Pilot 1000, popularizing the handheld organizer and establishing the software habits — Contacts, Calendar, Memos — that would carry through every device after it.

2003

Palm Treo 600

The first Treo combines Palm's organizer software with a phone and a QWERTY keyboard, setting the direction the line would follow.

2004

Palm Treo 650

A refined follow-up with a sharper display, a faster processor, expandable storage and a removable battery — the version of the Treo most people remember.

2005–2009

Later Treo Models

Palm continues the line with newer Treo models before the smartphone market shifts toward the touch-first devices that followed.
